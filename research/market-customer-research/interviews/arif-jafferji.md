## Key Commercial Insights

### 1. Diligency's commercial DD is customer-feedback-led but deliberately shallow on product depth

Arif's own workstream centres on structured customer interviews, assessing churn risk, relationship health, and competitive positioning, then linking findings back to market context (e.g., benchmarking a target's offering against named competitors). He is explicit that he will surface customer feedback like "your UX is out of date" but won't investigate why — that root-cause, product-level depth is outside his and his partners' remit, which is the same gap Lean Otter's product/UX-led diligence is built to fill.

### 2. Referral flow works in both directions

Arif thinks aloud through two concrete paths for collaboration: (1) Diligency completes commercial and technical DD, finds an issue that needs deeper product/UX investigation than they can provide, and refers or brings in Lean Otter for that next-level work; (2) Lean Otter is already advising a founder pre-exit or pre-raise, and Diligency provides a lighter-touch commercial/technical/operational "readiness" layer on top — management interviews, documentation review, pointers on what investors or acquirers will ask — that Arif described as relatively light effort on his side.

### 3. A live, concrete test case exists in Brom's current pipeline

Brom is advising a founder in HR tech targeting an exit in roughly a year who failed a raise/exit process two years prior; product issues were part of that failure, but so were unaddressed commercial gaps. Both agreed this is a strong fit for the "readiness" collaboration model discussed.

### 4. Diligency is also building an investor-facing "teaser"/positioning service, sized around $5m+ raises

Beyond due diligence, Diligency is developing a service that connects businesses seeking investment to investors and writes the positioning documentation in between — market positioning, use of capital, investment narrative. Arif said this tends to work for raises above roughly $5m, and confirmed Brom's HR tech client's $5–10m raise target fits that range.

### 5. Investors are increasingly sceptical of AI-wrapper products, which is squeezing that segment of the market

Arif's view, echoed independently by Brom's own observation of the Oxford startup scene, is that investors are "wising up" to thin AI-wrapper products with no real moat — functionality that was differentiated two to three years ago (e.g., "AI that builds your slides") is now table-stakes inside foundation model products themselves, and businesses built solely around that kind of feature are increasingly seen as non-scalable and are being valued accordingly.
