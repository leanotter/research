# Fergus Brownlee

## Meeting Details

- **Date:** Aug 11, 2026
- **Participants:** Gareth Jones, Fergus Brownlee
- **Meeting title:** Fergus

## Snapshot

This call was a working session between Gareth Jones and Fergus Brownlee, Chairman of Thomas International, where Gareth road-tested the pitch for the product/tech due-diligence and value-creation advisory business he is building with a US partner (George, of Worktech). Rather than a formal research interview, it played out as an experienced non-exec/chairman giving Gareth blunt commercial and go-to-market feedback: the underlying thesis, that DD processes are built around a legacy tech-only checklist and miss whether a business can actually execute product and go-to-market, is a "no brainer" that needs a much sharper pitch and needs to be blasted broadly across PE/VC targets rather than pursued one relationship at a time.

Fergus's most concrete input was tactical go-to-market advice (using a Vegas investor event as a lead-gen wedge, mass-outreach to an existing ~650-name PE/VC/investor list, and an expectation that Tier 1 funds will claim they already have it covered while mid-market UK funds genuinely don't), plus a handful of specific relationship introductions back into the Palamon/Thomas network (Jean, Anthony, Alistair/Luke, Adam). It reinforces, from a third independent angle, the same structural gap Sabby Gill and Matthew Hewlett described separately: DD is built by financial and technical people and stops at "is the stack any good," with almost nobody digging into whether product and go-to-market execution actually delivers value, pre- or post-deal.

## Who They Are / Relevant Context

- Still holds equity in Thomas ("I've got equity, I've never valued it as anything since day one"), so retains a personal financial stake in how the business performs.

## Key Commercial Insights

### 1. The DD "system" is dated but not fundamentally broken — it's missing a layer

Fergus's framing (prompted by Gareth) was that PE firms either run DD entirely in-house or lean on external advisers/investment bankers (he cited Golden Hill Capital and DC Advisory as examples used historically), and those advisers are financial and deal experts, not product or execution specialists. His conclusion: "it's not like the system's wrong, it's just that there's that depth that's missing" — validating Gareth's pitch that this is a genuine, addable layer rather than a replacement for existing DD.

### 2. Tier 1 funds will claim they have it covered — most mid-market UK funds don't

He was direct that the very top PE firms may genuinely have this covered internally, but the broader population of London-based mid-market funds will reflexively say "we've got it covered" while, in his view, factually lacking the depth Gareth is describing. That is a useful segmentation signal for who is worth pursuing versus who will waste time in the sales process.

### 3. Go big and blast broadly rather than cherry-pick a handful of targets

Drawing on his own experience raising funding for a charity (United World Schools) by targeting the entire PE firm list rather than three or four hand-picked names, Fergus pushed Gareth to mass-outreach his ~650-name PE/VC/investor list rather than relying only on warm one-to-one introductions, on the logic that a broad blast will still surface enough genuine interest to be worth it.

### 4. A live event (Vegas) is a stronger wedge than cold outreach alone

His single most concrete tactical suggestion was to use the Vegas HR tech show investor session Gareth is already running with George as an explicit lure: invite targeted PE/VC/investor contacts to attend, meet in person, and get a feel for the offer, rather than pitching cold over email or LinkedIn.

### 5. Underlying diagnosis matches what we're hearing elsewhere: DD stops at "is the stack any good"

Fergus's unprompted framing — "the tech, the deal bit, they're experts in that... the money bit you're experts in, not the DD bit" — and his agreement that DD is a "tick box" exercise that checks the stack but not execution, echoes almost exactly the gap Sabby Gill and Matthew Hewlett independently described: technical/architecture DD is mature, product and go-to-market execution DD is not. See [[sabby-gill]] and [[matthew-hewlett]].

## How This Market Buys

- PE firms either run DD fully in-house or route it through specialist deal/investment-banking advisers (e.g. Golden Hill Capital, DC Advisory), who are financial and transactional in focus, not product specialists.
- Sourcing/shortlisting quality from those advisers is inconsistent — Gareth's own experience at Thomas was that the shortlist process "was horrible... terrible," even from firms with strong deal-execution capability.
- The most credible entry point Fergus identified is volume outreach plus an in-person event touchpoint, not a slow, relationship-only approach, particularly for a new entrant without an established track record in the space yet.
- Expect polarised responses: some smaller/mid-market funds will engage seriously; the largest, most established London funds are more likely to reflexively deflect ("we've got it covered").

## Where Our Offer Fits

- Reinforces the same white space identified by Sabby Gill and Matthew Hewlett: product/go-to-market execution diligence sitting alongside, not replacing, standard tech DD.
- Gareth's own advisory venture (product/tech advisory plus investor-side DD and value creation, being built with US partner George) is materially the same thesis we are testing for Lean Otter, and is already road-tested on two live client projects — a useful proof point and internal reference case for our own positioning.
- The go-to-market lessons here (mass outreach over cherry-picking, using an event as a wedge, expecting deflection from the largest funds) are directly transferable to how we approach our own PE/adviser outreach.
- Value creation across a PE portfolio ("if you're a SaaS investor with 20 companies, you're probably not realising value across all of them in terms of some commonality or structure") is a second, related opportunity area beyond single-deal DD.

## Diligence / Value Creation Priorities

- Standard DD from investment-banking-style advisers covers financial/deal terms and a technical "is the stack any good" check — described as tick-box and dated, built for an era when tech was complex and custom rather than standardised and cloud-based.
- The real value-at-risk sits in execution: can the business actually convert a good tech stack into shipped product and go-to-market results — the same "rubber hits the road" theme Gareth raised independently in the [[matthew-hewlett]] conversation.
- Gareth cited external research suggesting roughly a third of deal failures trace back to this execution gap, reinforcing the scale of the opportunity.
- Cross-portfolio value creation (finding commonality and structure across multiple portfolio companies for a SaaS-focused PE/VC investor) is a related, largely unaddressed opportunity.

## Positioning And Messaging Implications

- Position clearly as additive to existing DD (financial/technical), not competitive with it — "the money bit you're experts in, not the DD bit" is a clean, borrowable framing.
- Lead with the "DD was built for a different tech era" narrative: cloud and standard platforms have made the technical check largely commoditised, while product execution and go-to-market delivery are where real risk now sits.
- Anchor messaging in a credible ROI/failure-rate statistic (roughly a third of deals failing on execution-related issues) rather than a purely qualitative pitch.
- Be ready for a segment of the market, especially the largest, most established funds, to reflexively claim they already have this covered; don't over-invest in convincing that segment first.

## GTM / Relationship Strategy

- Consider an event-led wedge similar to the Vegas HR tech show investor session: a live touchpoint where target PE/VC/investor contacts can meet the team and understand the offer, rather than relying on cold outreach alone.
- Favour broad, blasted outreach across a full target list (Gareth's own list runs to roughly 650 PE/VC/investor contacts) over narrowly hand-picking a small number of warm introductions — Fergus's charity-fundraising experience suggests breadth converts better than expected.
- Specific relationship introductions surfaced in this call, via the Palamon/Thomas network: Jean (now heads the Palamon board), Anthony (board member, financial PR background, runs Green Target, ex-Citigate), Alistair/Luke (chair/CEO-level figures), and Adam (outgoing Thomas exec, believed to be moving into the music industry, described as well-connected).
- Guy Ballantine (already a known contact of ours, see [[guy-ballantine]]) was independently flagged again here as "grounded" and a useful second-opinion sounding board — a second, independent confirmation that he is a good relationship to keep warm.

## Commercial Model Considerations

- Gareth's structure, two independent businesses ("Worktech Solutions" in the UK/Europe, George's "Worktech" in the US) trading under closely aligned branding, with commercial terms worked out deal-by-deal behind the scenes, is a lightweight, low-risk model worth being aware of as a reference structure.
- His delivery model uses independent contractors with their own businesses rather than employees, keeping the cost base and risk low while still scaling delivery capacity.
- Monetisation for the event/GTM wedge is modest and secondary (ticket-revenue-shared investor sessions functioning mainly as an awareness and relationship-building loss-leader, not a primary income line).

## Risks / Challenges

- The largest, most established PE funds are likely to be the hardest segment to convert, since they will assert, rightly or wrongly, that they already have this covered internally.
- Broad-blast outreach risks diluting a young, unproven offer if the pitch and messaging aren't tightly aligned across all outbound touchpoints — Fergus explicitly flagged the need for message consistency ("two jaws... need to be aligned up").
- Gareth's own venture, while directionally identical to our thesis, is a separate legal entity and brand (Worktech Solutions / Worktech), so it is worth clarifying overlap, differentiation, and any coordination with our own Lean Otter positioning as both efforts mature.
- This particular call was informal and relationship-based rather than a structured research interview, so some of Fergus's specific factual claims (fund names, deal mechanics, historical detail) are anecdotal and would benefit from independent verification before being relied on directly.
