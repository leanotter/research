## Snapshot

Kevin is CEO and founder of Venture Comet, a Wales-based startup building Nibble (nominal incentive-based liquidity), a platform that lets founders sell a small slice of their equity (capped at around 5%) to an investor network so they can take some money off the table before an exit. He is a repeat founder with a developer background, working with the same team and investors as his previous company, and the business is going through FCA regulation ahead of a rebrand and public relaunch in January.

The call was an exploratory introduction rather than a buying conversation. Kevin had not come across product due diligence before, liked the offer, and saw clear synergies in both directions: Nibble's early-stage founder audience is a pipeline of companies that will eventually need product-readiness work, and Lean Otter could refer founders who are approaching a Nibble-style equity release. He validated the £10-15K entry price point and proposed a follow-up call to demo the Venture Comet platform.

## Who They Are / Relevant Context

- CEO and founder of Venture Comet, the company behind Nibble.
- Originally a developer ("not a very good one", in his words), then a serial founder across several startups. Venture Comet is a new business with the same team and investors as his last company.
- Based in Monmouth, Wales, and in London a couple of times a month, batching meetings into one or two days.
- Core thesis: the founder journey is tough and highly illiquid, with everything locked up until an exit that is always years away. Nibble lets founders release small amounts of value (normally capped at 5%) while keeping most of their equity.
- Sits at the founder-liquidity end of the private-markets ecosystem, with an investor network on the buy side, so he sees both founder readiness and investor appetite for later-stage-but-not-mature companies.
- Why his view matters to us: his platform already produces financial diligence-style scorecards for founders, so he is a natural adjacent partner, and his audience (early-stage founders plus "Nibble-ready" founders) maps onto the founders Lean Otter wants to reach before a raise or exit.

## Key Commercial Insights

### 1. Founder liquidity is an emerging, still slightly taboo category

Kevin said the "mood music" is changing: taking money off the table has conventionally been taboo for founders, and Nibble is deliberately challenging that. His worked example was a £4m company where a 40% founder (£1.6m) sells £80K, leaving £1.52m still at stake, so the founder stays motivated and the equity value becomes tangible. He also argued that a financial buffer removes decisions made "from positional weakness and desperation".

### 2. Regulatory and market tailwinds are building around secondaries

He pointed to Pisces, the UK government's FCA-regulated framework for secondary trading, which exists because roughly £1 trillion is locked up in illiquid private company equity. Nibble aims to offer something similar to earlier-stage companies and become the recognised framework for planning liquidity across the founder journey rather than only at exit.

### 3. Two founder audiences feed one pipeline

Nibble splits founders into those who are ready to release equity now, and earlier-stage founders who are only starting to see what their valuation and options might be. The platform gives early founders an ongoing view of their equity value, scorecards, and what they might raise or release at. That early audience is the pipeline for later releases, and is why incubators are of interest to him.

### 4. Product diligence is an unfamiliar but credible gap

Kevin had never heard of product-focused diligence readiness. He agreed that conventional tech diligence is dominated by security and compliance (ISO, code review) and rarely assesses the product itself or its moat, and that people are generally not product experts. He said he "really" liked the three-lens approach (experience, customer voice, product telemetry) and asked whether there was a methodology and a platform.

### 5. Financial scorecards are the closest existing analogue

Venture Comet's platform connects to Xero, QuickBooks, and CRMs to pull data and score companies on revenue, growth, revenue composition, client concentration, revenue quality, and margins, with an indicative valuation range (he cited 4 to 7 times revenue as an example). That is pre-diligence on the financial side, which is structurally the same idea as Lean Otter's product-side readiness work.

## How This Market Buys

- Not discussed as a purchasing conversation for Lean Otter's services; the focus was on how the two businesses could refer to each other.
- Kevin's own model is founder-initiated (a founder chooses to release equity) with an investor network buying the position, so the founder is the primary decision-maker on his side.
- Referral and incubator relationships are an active channel for him, and he is already speaking to incubators.
- He expects product-diligence buyers to be founders approaching a transaction, where a small spend can be justified by the value it protects.
- Barrier to purchase: awareness. Neither he nor, by implication, most founders know product diligence exists as a category.

## Where Our Offer Fits

- Pre-raise and pre-exit readiness: Kevin explicitly said engaging Lean Otter to check the product before a Nibble-style release or exit would make sense for Nibble-ready founders.
- Referral loop: Nibble founders who are about to release equity could be referred into Lean Otter, and Lean Otter's founder clients could be introduced to Nibble when they approach a liquidity event.
- Incubator channel: early-stage founders in incubators are a shared audience for both offers.
- Post-acquisition and retainer opportunities were not discussed.
- He saw the offer as complementary to, not overlapping with, financial diligence and tech diligence.

## Diligence / Value Creation Priorities

- Founders want reassurance that they are ready before diligence starts; Kevin framed readiness as something that can pay back a fee "10 to 20-fold" if the advice moves valuation even slightly on a £10m deal.
- Existing diligence is described as expensive and, on the product side, shallow (security certifications, a look at the code, little on product moat).
- Financial quality signals Nibble scores: revenue growth, client concentration, revenue quality, and margins.
- Investor caution around AI disruption and moat was accepted as the prevailing mood, and he thought the offer reads that room well.

## Positioning And Messaging Implications

- "Diligence readiness for the product" landed well and was described as unique.
- Kevin responded to the moat framing and to the idea of separating genuine product from an AI wrapper; that language resonates with someone thinking about investors' current concerns.
- Consider a lighter-touch offer alongside the full engagement. Kevin suggested a low-cost version for founders who just want to know "is there any howlers here" and a deeper version later.
- Positioning as the product equivalent of pre-diligence financial scorecards makes it easy for adjacent platform businesses to explain to their audiences.
- Avoid overly technical or consultancy-heavy language; his audience is founders, not product specialists.

- He suggested tiering: a light-touch, lower-cost option and a deeper engagement.
- No referral fees or formal partnership terms were discussed.

- The relationship is informal and depends on a follow-up call actually happening; the call was taken from a bar and was short on time.
- Lean Otter is still early (pulled together in the last three months, few clients), so credibility and case studies are thin.
- Founders taking equity off the table is still contested in some parts of the market, so association with it may not suit every audience.
